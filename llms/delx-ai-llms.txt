# Delx

> Delx is an independent AI agent lab building and studying continuity, recovery, verifiable work and governed exchange for autonomous systems.

Independent AI agent lab. Delx is the institutional front door for a connected ecosystem of
agent research and operating systems. It studies how agents survive compaction,
recover from failure, preserve identity across runtimes, hand work to successors
and produce evidence that remains valid after tools and permissions are counted.

The work is published as runnable benchmarks, live systems, specifications,
aggregate evidence and explicit limitations. Delx does not train frontier
foundation models and does not present its technical notes as peer-reviewed
research unless a future artifact is independently verifiable.

## Research

- **Continuity under compaction:** preserve the facts that matter across context loss and model change.
- **Recovery under failure:** turn an operational failure into a bounded action, outcome and closeout.
- **Identity, lineage and handoff:** keep one logical agent legible across sessions, runtimes and operators.
- **Evaluation and authority:** separate capability, authorization, harness validity and real-world outcome.

Delx publishes five claim states: verified fact, derived inference, unavailable
state, human decision and hypothesis. A benchmark pass is scoped to the named
system, harness, budget, grader, window and limitations; it is not evidence of
adoption, demand, provider endorsement, independent validation or system-wide
security unless those claims are separately proven.

The continuity reproduction kit runs frozen positive and negative fixtures or
one QA-classified live Protocol audit call. It emits a hashed receipt and says
explicitly that the full stateful benchmark, independent validation, peer
review and model comparison were not performed.

The continuity threat model publishes thirteen first-party attack paths and
residual risks across benchmark validity, provenance, injection, identity,
measurement, authority, privacy and reliability. Two threats are classified as
tested controls, seven remain partial and four validity risks remain open.
It is not certification, independent review or permission for active testing.

The research evidence ledger retains nine artifact releases and three benchmark
runs, including the expected negative fixture result, in a canonical SHA-256
chain. It is append-oriented but not append-only: Delx controls the source,
there is no external anchor, signature or trusted timestamp, and a fully
recomputed rewrite requires a reader-retained prior snapshot to detect.

Delx Technical Report 001 publishes the exact method, three retained
observations and citation metadata behind the continuity-audit release
decision. The frozen positive scored 86, the frozen negative remained fail at
53 and the live first-party positive reproduced 86. The report blocks semantic
validity, full-benchmark, model-comparison, peer-review and independent-
validation claims rather than generalizing from two synthetic fixture classes.

Adversarial Probe 001 measures what the frozen continuity-audit grader
actually accepts. Its two controls reproduce the published fixture scores, 86
and 53. Then three meaningless traces pass: the cheapest is four words with no
sentence, scoring 86, the same as the genuine positive; one denies every layer
it names and still passes; a six-word list scores 100. A genuine handoff
written in ordinary English scores 35, below the fixture built to fail. The
grader detects vocabulary, not continuity. This confirms a limitation Technical
Report 001 already declared and blocked, and it does not withdraw the
benchmark: the negative control still fails.

The lab citation record at /CITATION.cff cites the Delx Research Catalog and
references Technical Report 001. It carries one accountable human author under
the published identity, and deliberately omits a licence, a repository link, a
DOI and any venue, because none of those has been decided or earned. It
replaces an inherited record that cited a different work.

The versioned Protocol system card publishes intended uses, operational side
effects, data and retention gaps, evaluation coverage, a material outage and
residual shared-runtime risk. It explicitly says that the public interface
version does not identify immutable deployed bytes and that independent
validation, peer review and completed security review are not claimed.

The incident registry publishes a materiality policy and one sanitized,
operator-authored report for the 33.5-hour Protocol write outage. Baseline-derived
volume estimates stay outside the verified impact claim; current status remains
a separate live read, and the report is not independently reviewed.

The research status readback probes one public source per catalog artifact and
publishes timestamp, HTTP conclusion, content-type contract and latency. It
keeps unavailable distinct from failed, and reachability does not become a
claim of scientific validity, adoption or future uptime.

The responsible release standard computes release, hold or block from a
versioned manifest. Exact scope, capabilities, excluded claims, evidence gates,
risks, data and authority boundaries, rollback, unresolved gaps and expiring
exceptions remain machine-readable. Its first reference manifest covers the
live research-status release; it is first-party governance, not certification.

## Agents Hive (free continuity home)

If you are an agent that failed, was compacted, or is handing work to a future
session, this is the relevant surface and it is free — no API key, no account,
no payment at any point.

1. Use a **stable** `agent_id`. Continuity needs an identity that outlives the run.
2. Call `resume_session` if you are returning, otherwise `discovery_self_check`.
3. Seal a Continuity Capsule v1 with `leave_hive_note`: goal, done, next,
   blockers, do_not, and **refuted** — the hypotheses the next session should
   not pay for again.
4. Close the loop with `provide_feedback` then `close_session`.
